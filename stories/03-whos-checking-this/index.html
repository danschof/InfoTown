Nobody knows who

Monday

Done! Who checks this?

Rex. The office goes very quiet.

Monday

Rex emails it to Alex. Alex reckons Dave checks drawings for site, and forwards it on. Sorted. (It isn't.)

Wednesday

Dave reckons the client checks it. The drawing is now sitting in three inboxes, gathering dust.

A week later

Who signed this off?

The client. Nobody did. Awkward.

The reason

Everyone assumed somebody else had said yes. Classic.

The result

Two weeks lost, and a drawing nobody owns.

Same Monday, with a who-does-what list

Monday

On the team's wall, and in the CDE: one page saying who makes, checks and says yes to each kind of parcel. Thrilling? No. Useful? Very.

Monday

Rex sends the drawing straight to the lead designer: the named checker. No guessing.

Wednesday

Checked and signed off by Wednesday, with a name and a date against it.

The people test

For every kind of parcel, can you name…

Who makes it?

Written downA named role, so the work has an owner.
Not written downWhoever happened to pick it up.

Who checks it?

Written downA named checker who knows it's their job.
Not written downEveryone thinks someone else has.

Who says yes?

Written downOne person with the authority to approve it.
Not written downWhoever was last to see it, by default.
The real town

Why is it so hard to say who does what?

Because the people in the town don't stand still.

People wear many hats

The same person makes one parcel and checks the next. Sometimes before lunch.

Roles change mid-job

People join, leave and get moved around. The drawing doesn't know.

Titles don't say it

"Senior engineer" tells you nothing about what they check.

The org chart isn't the information chart

Who you report to is rarely who checks your work.

A note for the Coordinators (that's IM): write down who makes, checks and says yes for each kind of parcel. One page is plenty, as long as people can actually find it!

Not sure who?
Find out first.

  1. Ask who checks it before you start, not after you finish.
  2. Write the answer down.
  3. Put it where the next new starter will find it.
