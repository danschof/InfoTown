Nobody asks what's needed

Week 1

Right, we're building a pumping station. Off you go!

The client. Lots of nodding. Nobody writes anything down.

Weeks 1–2

I'll start with the roof detail. It's the fun bit.

Alex, designer. Two weeks of genuinely lovely work.

Week 3

Where's the drainage layout? We start digging Monday!

Dave, site manager, on the phone, getting louder.

Week 3

Drainage? Nobody said anything about drainage.

Alex. Technically correct. Not helpful.

Six months later

Where are the inspection records for the drainage?

The Clerk of Works. Nobody planned any inspections, so there aren't any.

The result

A beautiful roof detail nobody needed yet, a drainage drawing done in a panic, and no proof anything was built right.

Same project, starting with the question

Week 1

What are we building? Right, that means 12 drawings. Let's plan it.

The design team turns the client's need into a list. The roof detail can wait.

Week 1

And we'll need inspections to prove we built it right.

Dave, planning the proof before he's dug a single hole.

Week 1, Thursday

Alex opens the Design System and starts on 042, the drainage layout. Needed, planned, then made. That's how Drawing 042 is born.

The need test

Before you make anything, can you answer…

Who needs it?

Written downA named person or team is waiting for it.
In someone's head"I just assumed someone would want it."

What for?

Written downIt answers a question or lets someone do a job.
In someone's head"It seemed like a good idea at the time."

By when?

Written downA date that fits the work that depends on it.
In someone's head"ASAP." So, never, then everything at once.
The real town

Why do people start making before anyone's asked?

Because making things is the fun part, and planning feels like a delay.

Everyone's itching to start

Designing beats writing lists. Every time.

The client assumes you know

And you assume they'll tell you.

"It's in the contract somewhere"

Page 214. Nobody has read page 214.

Proof is an afterthought

Until someone asks for it, six months later.

A note for the Coordinators (that's IM): start every project with the client's question: what are we building, and what do we need to prove it? Then turn the answers into a list everyone can actually see!

Already making things?
Check the need.

  1. Ask what the client actually needs from you.
  2. Write the list: what, who, by when.
  3. Add the inspections and records you'll need to prove it.
