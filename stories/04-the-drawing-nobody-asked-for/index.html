The drawing nobody asked for

A new pumping station. A keen design team. A site team about to start digging. What could go wrong?

Before you start

So far

In Lesson 4 you saw how the client's question becomes a recipe, and why nothing gets made unless it's on it. This team skipped that bit.

Nobody asks what's needed

Week 1

The client: Right, we're building a pumping station. Off you go!

Lots of nodding. Nobody writes anything down.

Weeks 1–2

Alex: I'll start with the roof detail. It's the fun bit.

Two weeks of genuinely lovely work.

Week 3

Dave: Where's the drainage layout? We start digging Monday!

On the phone, getting louder.

Week 3

Alex: Drainage? Nobody said anything about drainage.

Technically correct. Not helpful.

Six months later

The Clerk of Works: Where are the inspection records for the drainage?

Nobody planned any inspections, so there aren't any.

The result

A beautiful roof detail nobody needed yet, a drainage drawing done in a panic, and no proof anything was built right.

Same project, starting with the question

Week 1

The design team: What are we building? Right, that means 12 drawings. Let's plan it.

Turning the client's need into a list. The roof detail can wait.

Week 1

Dave: And we'll need inspections to prove we built it right.

Planning the proof before he's dug a single hole.

Week 1, Thursday

Alex opens the Design System and starts on 042, the drainage layout. Needed, planned, then made. That's how the drainage drawing is born.
