Who's who in town

Meet the locals

The people you'll bump into in the lessons and stories, who they work for, and what ISO 19650 would call them.

The main contractor

Tier 1

A big contractor leading the job: the Lead Appointed Party. Rex's firm.

Rex

Assistant engineer. New starter.

Works for
A big Tier 1 contractor: the Lead Appointed Party on this job
In ISO terms
A task team member.
What he does
Calcs, site records, and asking "where does this go?" so you don't have to.

A few weeks in and he's already found a folder called OLD STUFF and used it. Learning fast, mostly the hard way. You can't miss him: the hair escaping from his hard hat, and the orange vest.

Meet him in Story 1, then follow him through his whole first year.

Sam

Information manager. The Coordinator.

Works for
The same Tier 1 as Rex
In ISO terms
The information management function, for the Lead Appointed Party
What they do
Doesn't place the order or write the parts list: the client says what they need and the teams work out what goes in. Sam keeps it all written down and joined up, builds the Site Office, puts up the signposts, writes the rulebook and plans the handover.

When Sam gets it right, nobody notices. The only person on site who has read the BEP, and possibly the only one who knows there is one. Gives advice in the orange boxes. Whether you asked or not.

Follow Sam's own journey, from the tender to audit day. Look out for Sam's Heads Up (tap to open) on every lesson.

Dave

Site manager.

Works for
The Tier 1, running the site
In ISO terms
A user of information: the person everything else is for
What he does
Builds things. Ideally from the right drawing. Historically from whatever's in the van door.

Thirty years on site. Phone currently in an excavation. Buys the teas when it goes right, which is happening more often than it used to.

Meet him in Story 1, then in most of the stories, especially The drawing in the van.

Helen

Divisional director. The Town Hall.

Works for
The same Tier 1 as Rex
In ISO terms
Senior leadership of the lead appointed party: accountable for what the firm promises it can do
What they do
Decides which jobs to bid for, owns the big risks, and signs off what the firm commits to.

Her phone only ever rings with bad news. Keeps a cake on standby, just in case.

Follow her own journey from Helen's Story 1. She also takes a call in Rex's Story 10.

Kofi

Commercial manager. Commercial.

Works for
The same Tier 1 as Rex
In ISO terms
Not named in the standard, but prices and agrees the appointments it relies on
What they do
Prices the work, agrees the changes, and counts what every shortcut costs.

Has never agreed a change without the paperwork. Or a fight. Tears his hair out when information wasn't in the price.

Meet him in Rex's Story 8 and Sam's Tender stories 7 and 8, or follow his own journey.

Tom

The same Tier 1 as Rex

Gets the bid out of the door, on time, every time. Somehow.

Every deadline is tomorrow. The stopwatch isn't decorative.

Meet him all through Sam's tender, from Tender story 1, or follow his own journey.

Stevie

QHSE manager. Assurance.

Works for
The same Tier 1 as Rex
In ISO terms
Owns the quality system the information process sits inside

The Checker

Whoever's checking this one.

Works for
Usually the same team as the maker. Never the maker.
In ISO terms
Carries out the quality assurance check before work is shared
What they do
Makes sure it's made properly: the right label, template and standard.

Deliberately has no name. Checking is a job, not a person, and on a good team it gets passed around. On a bad team it gets passed around too, just never done.

Client Town

Who the work is for: the Appointing Party.

Emilia

The client's new information manager.

Works for
Client Town: the Appointing Party
In ISO terms
The appointing party's information management function

Owns the client's information requirements, looks after the client's side of the CDE, and decides with the client's team what gets accepted.

Has read the first sixty pages of the requirements template. Has started a glossary. The glossary is now longer than the template.

Her own journey is coming in Phase 4. You can already pick her on the world map.

The Clerk of Works

The client's eyes on site.

Works for
Client Town: the Appointing Party
In ISO terms
Acting for the appointing party: reviews the work, and advises on acceptance

Not smiling. Occasionally nearly smiling. Asks "built to which revision?" in a tone that makes grown engineers check their pockets.

The travellers

Not people, but they travel further than any of them. One need becomes a plan, the plan becomes parcels, and the parcels become what the owner runs the asset from.

The need

A pumping station, because the old one keeps flooding.

Belongs to
The client, starting in its Town Hall
In ISO terms
Organisational, asset and project information requirements, written down for the job as exchange information requirements
What it does
Starts as a question, becomes a project, then becomes the order every firm prices.

Usually first said out loud in a meeting where nobody was taking notes. Except Rex.

See it become a project: Helen's Story 1, then Lesson 4, What have they asked for?

The plan

The parts list: every deliverable, who makes it and when.

Belongs to
Each task team writes its own; the lead firm joins them into one
In ISO terms
Task and master information delivery plans (TIDP, MIDP)
What it does
Turns the order into planned deliverables, lined up with the programme.

Most useful when somebody opens it after the tender. Ideally weekly.

See it become planned deliverables: Lesson 4, then Sam's Delivery 3, Who makes what, and when

An information container

The handover

The pack the owner runs the pumping station from.

Belongs to
The client, and the people who operate and maintain the asset
In ISO terms
The asset information model, with health and safety information (ISO 19650-6)
What it does
Carries the as-built record, O&M manuals, asset data and the health and safety file into years of use.

The only traveller that never gets to retire. The maintenance crew will still be opening it in twenty years.

See it taken on and used: Lesson 10 and Lesson 11, Bev's product data, Priya's safety file

Sam's Heads Up

These are roles, not job titles

ISO 19650 talks about parties, teams and functions, not job titles like site manager or designer.

So when someone says "send it to the lead appointed party", don't look for a person with that on their badge. Look at your project's who-does-what list. It'll tell you exactly who that means on this job.
