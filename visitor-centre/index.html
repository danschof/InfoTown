Who's who in town

Meet the locals

The people you'll bump into in the lessons and stories, who they work for, and what ISO 19650 would call them.

Rex

Assistant engineer. New starter.

Works for
A big Tier 1 contractor: the Lead Appointed Party on this job
In ISO terms
A task team member. A Maker.
What he does
Calcs, site records, and asking "where does this go?" so you don't have to.

Three weeks in and he's already found a folder called OLD STUFF and used it. Learning fast, mostly the hard way. You can't miss him: the hair, and the orange tie.

Meet him in Lesson 1, 2, 3 and 5, and most of the stories.

Sam

Information manager. The Coordinator.

Works for
The same Tier 1 as Rex
In ISO terms
The information management function, for the Lead Appointed Party
What they do
Doesn't write the recipe: the client says what they need and the teams work out what goes in. Sam keeps it all written down and joined up, builds the Site Office, puts up the signposts, writes the rulebook and plans the handover.

When Sam gets it right, nobody notices. The only person on site who has read the BEP, and possibly the only one who knows there is one. Gives advice in the orange boxes. Whether you asked or not.

Look out for Sam's Heads Up in Lessons 5, 7, 9 and 10.

Alex

Designer.

Works for
A design consultant in Designer Town: an Appointed Party
In ISO terms
A task team member. The originator of Drawing 042.
What they do
Draws Drawing 042, and occasionally 39 of its friends in one PDF.

Thinks "Everything for Area 2.pdf" is a gift. Has never met a deadline they couldn't hit at 4:55 on a Friday.

Meet them in Lesson 6, 7, 8 and 9.

Dave

Site manager.

Works for
The Tier 1, running the site
In ISO terms
A user of information: the person everything else is for
What he does
Builds things. Ideally from the right drawing. Historically from whatever's in the van door.

Thirty years on site. Phone currently in an excavation. Buys the teas when it goes right, which is happening more often than it used to.

Meet him in Lesson 3 and Lesson 11, and in most of the stories.

The Clerk of Works

The client's eyes on site.

Works for
Client Town: the Appointing Party
In ISO terms
Acting for the appointing party, reviewing and accepting
What they do
Accepts things. Or doesn't.

Not smiling. Occasionally nearly smiling. Asks "built to which revision?" in a tone that makes grown engineers check their pockets.

Meet them in Lesson 10 and the stories for Lessons 4, 6, 8, 10 and 11.

The Checker

Whoever's checking this one.

Works for
Usually the same team as the maker. Never the maker.
In ISO terms
Carries out the quality assurance check before work is shared
What they do
Makes sure it's made properly: the right label, template and standard.

Deliberately has no name. Checking is a job, not a person, and on a good team it gets passed around. On a bad team it gets passed around too, just never done.

Meet them in Lesson 9 and its story, The rubber stamp.

Drawing 042

A drainage layout for Area 2.

Belongs to
Alex made it, the project owns it, the client keeps it
In ISO terms
An information container
What it does
Gets made, labelled, shared, checked, accepted, built from, and kept.

Has been emailed, WhatsApped, rubber-stamped, glued into a PDF and left in a van. Remarkably, it's still going.

Follow it from Lesson 6 to Lesson 12, or watch the whole thing in Follow the Process.

Sam's Heads Up

These are roles, not job titles

ISO 19650 never mentions site managers or designers. It talks about parties, teams and functions. The same person can be a Maker on Monday, a Checker on Tuesday and a User by Friday.

So when someone says "send it to the lead appointed party", don't look for a person with that on their badge. Look at your project's who-does-what list. It'll tell you exactly who that means on this job.
