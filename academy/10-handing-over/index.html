The scene

From the shared table to the published shelf

Parcels on the shared table carry S codes: shared, and fit for something. To become published, they make one more trip. The firm leading the work authorises the delivery, the client accepts it, and the S becomes an A. If it isn't right, it comes back with comments. And some parcels never make the trip at all. Sam's Heads Up

S and A codes are the UK way, not the only way

The S1 to S4 and A codes here come from the UK National Annex to ISO 19650. Plenty of clients use them. Plenty don't, and some invent new ones halfway through the job.

Check your project's status codes before you share, authorise or build anything. "Approved" on one job can mean "please don't build this yet" on another.

The InfoTown idea

Shared isn't finished. The firm leading the work authorises it, the client accepts it, and only then is it published. Not everything goes to the client.

The reveal

This is Authorisation & Acceptance!

Moving information from shared to published: authorised by the firm leading the work, then accepted by the client.

In ISO 19650 the lead appointed party reviews and authorises the information model (everything being handed over, taken together) for submission, then the appointing party reviews and accepts it, and it moves to the published state. In the UK, codes show the journey: S codes while it's shared, A codes once it's authorised and accepted. Only the information in the delivery plan needs to make the trip.
