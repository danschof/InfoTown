The scene

From the shared table to the published shelf

Parcels on the shared table carry S codes: shared, and fit for something. To become published, they make one more trip. The firm leading the work authorises the delivery, the client accepts it, and the S becomes an A. If it isn't right, it comes back with comments. And some parcels never make the trip at all. An S code says what something may be used for. An A code only says it's been signed off, and the n is the work stage. The Annex ties the stages to BS 8536-1 (buildings) and BS 8536-2 (infrastructure), which follow the familiar Stage 0 to 7 pattern of the RIBA Plan of Work, so on this site a construction issue is A5 and the as-built record is A6, unless your project's information standard sets its own. What each A code is issued for still has to be written in that standard.

Source: the UK National Annex to BS EN ISO 19650-2:2018 (Table NA.1, NA.3, Table NA.2 and NA.4.3), summarised in plain English. Your project's information standard can add codes to these, but shouldn't replace them.

Sam's Heads Up

S and A codes are the UK way, not the only way

The S0 to S5 and A codes here come from the UK National Annex to ISO 19650. Plenty of clients use them. Plenty don't, and some invent new ones halfway through the job.

Check your project's status codes before you share, authorise or build anything. "Approved" on one job can mean "please don't build this yet" on another.

The InfoTown idea

Shared isn't finished. The firm leading the work authorises it, the client accepts it, and only then is it published. Not everything goes to the client.

The reveal

This is Authorisation & Acceptance!

Moving information from shared to published: authorised by the firm leading the work, then accepted by the client.

In ISO 19650 the lead appointed party reviews and authorises the information model (everything being handed over, taken together) for submission, then the appointing party reviews and accepts it, and it moves to the published state. In the UK, status codes show the journey: S4 when it's shared for the lead to authorise, S5 when it's shared for the client to accept, then an A code once it's signed off, numbered for the work stage. Only the information in the delivery plan needs to make the trip.

  3. Agree with the client how they'll accept it, and write it down.

A note for the Coordinators (that's IM): plan the handover in the recipe on day one: what the client gets, when, who authorises it and how the client will accept it. Handover is a journey, not a dump truck!
