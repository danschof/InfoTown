    Sam's Heads Up

    One requirement, lots of parcels

    Notice how one need from the client spawns a whole family: the requirements, the plan, the drawing, the red-line, the inspection record, the as-built and the handover pack. Each one is its own parcel with its own number, and each one leaves its own trail. A real job runs thousands of these at once.

    The codes are the UK ones from the National Annex to ISO 19650, with A5 for construction and A6 for the as-built record. Your project's BEP, naming convention and status codes may differ, so check them.

    DeprecatedB1… BnAn old code for "signed off, with comments". No longer used–

    An S code says what something may be used for. An A code only says it's been signed off, and the n is the work stage. The Annex ties the stages to BS 8536-1 (buildings) and BS 8536-2 (infrastructure), which follow the familiar Stage 0 to 7 pattern of the RIBA Plan of Work, so on this site a construction issue is A5 and the as-built record is A6, unless your project's information standard sets its own. What each A code is issued for still has to be written in that standard.

    The codes on Drawing 042

    Form D is a drawing (T would be text, L a list or schedule). Discipline C is civil engineering (X means not discipline-specific). XX means a field isn't subdivided, ZZ means it covers several. The Annex has the full lists of form and discipline codes, and your project's information standard says which ones your job uses.
