The scene

Every town has two ways across it

The main road is the one we built. It goes through the Post Office, where every parcel is booked in. The back alley is the shortcut people take when they're in a hurry. Both get the parcel there.

The back alley test

Two questions for every parcel you send or receive

Can you find it later?

Main roadIt's in the CDE with its label. Anyone with access can find it, even after the sender has left the job.
Back alleyIt's in one person's phone, inbox or chat. When that person moves on, it goes with them.

Can you prove it?

Main roadThe CDE records who sent which version, when, and what state it was in.
Back alleyThe parcel arrived, but there's no record of what it was, who sent it or whether it was approved.
The real town

Why do people use back alleys?

Usually for a good reason. Back alleys aren't drawn on the official map, but every town has them.

It's quicker

The main road feels slow when the pour is on Monday.

They don't know the road

Nobody showed them how to send it properly.

The road is closed to them

They have no CDE access, or the right folder doesn't exist yet.

Everyone else does it

An alley used often enough starts to feel like the road.

A note for the town planners (that's IM): if lots of people use the same back alley, the road is probably in the wrong place. Find the busy alleys and fix the road.

Already in the alley?
Bring it back.

  1. Put the parcel on the CDE.
  2. Record the instruction: who sent it, what it was and when.
  3. Tell the team where to find it.
The InfoTown term

Every town has roads we built and back alleys people use. Both move parcels. Only one leaves a trail.

The reveal

This is an Information Exchange!

The back alley is an informal exchange, and informal exchanges are a risk.

ISO 19650 expects information to be shared through the CDE, so every container carries its status and revision and leaves a record of who shared it. The main road is that route. Anything that goes round it skips those protections.

Take it back to your project

Take-away question

Think of the last drawing or instruction you got. Could you find it in six months' time? Could you prove who sent it and which version it was?
