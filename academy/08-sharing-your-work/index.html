Every town has two ways across it

The exchange map

Which line, which parcels, and when

Every project needs a map of its proper routes: who sends what, to whom, and when. Drawings come in on the design line, go out to site on the site line, and up to the client when they need approving. If it's on the map, everyone knows where to look. The back alley never is. Want to see a real job ride it? Follow the Process.

The InfoTown idea

Every town has routes we built and back alleys people use. Both move parcels. Only one leaves a trail.

The reveal

This is an Information Exchange!

The back alley is an informal exchange, and informal exchanges are a risk.

ISO 19650 plans exchanges up front (who delivers what, to whom, at each milestone) and expects them to go through the CDE, so every container carries its status and revision and leaves a record of who shared it. That's the proper route. Anything that goes round it skips those protections.

The back alley test Two questions for every parcel you send or receive

Can you find it later?

Proper routeIt's in the CDE with its label. Anyone with access can find it, even after the sender has left the job.
Back alleyIt's in one person's phone, inbox or chat. When that person moves on, it goes with them.

Can you prove it?

Proper routeThe CDE records who sent which version, when, and what state it was in.
Back alleyThe parcel arrived, but there's no record of what it was, who sent it or whether it was approved.
The real town Why do people use back alleys?

Usually for a good reason. Back alleys aren't drawn on the official map, but every town has them.

It's quicker

The proper route feels painfully slow when the pour is on Monday.

They don't know the road

Nobody ever showed them how to send it properly.

The road is closed to them

No CDE access, or the folder they need doesn't exist yet.

Everyone else does it

Use an alley often enough and it starts to feel like the road.

Fix it Already in the alley? Bring it back.

Already in the alley?
Bring it back.

  1. Put the parcel on the CDE.
  2. Record the instruction: who sent it, what it was and when.
  3. Tell the team where to find it.

A note for the Coordinators (that's IM): if everyone's using the same back alley, the proper route is probably in the wrong place. Find the busy alleys and fix the route!

Think of the last drawing or instruction you got. Could you find it in six months' time? Could you prove who sent it and which version it was?
