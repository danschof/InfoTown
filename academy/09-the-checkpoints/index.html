Four checkpoints on the road out

Between the Design System and the shared table sit four checkpoints. The maker finishes it, someone else checks it's made properly, someone reviews whether it does the job, and someone with the authority approves it for sharing. Above the road hangs the rulebook that says how. Pick a checkpoint to see what happens there.

Sam's Heads Up

Four checkpoints is the minimum, not the maximum

Your company's check-and-approve process might have six steps, two signatures and a form nobody can find. That's fine, as long as the four here are in there somewhere and someone actually does them.

The rules for your job live in its BEP and your company's procedures. If they say something different from this page, they win. If they say nothing at all, that's a bigger problem.

The InfoTown idea

Every parcel passes four checkpoints before it's shared: made, checked, reviewed, approved. The rulebook says how.

The reveal

This is an Approval Process!

Originate, check, review, approve: a parcel is only shared once someone with the authority says it's right.

ISO 19650 expects a quality assurance check and a review against the information requirements before information is approved for sharing and moves from work in progress to the shared state. The BIM Execution Plan (BEP) records how the delivery team will do this, and a security-minded approach (ISO 19650-5) decides who gets to see what.
