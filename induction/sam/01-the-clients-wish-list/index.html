Think like Sam

Treat every "see Appendix…" and every document the tender mentions as a requirement until proven otherwise. Can you trace each one to a line on your list, and does every gap have either an answer from the client or an assumption in the bid?

The right way, explained

The right way, explained

The client's wish list is the order for the whole town. Before anyone builds a road, someone has to read the order. All of it, including the bit stapled to the back and the catalogue it points to.

If you're the client

If you're the client

Put every document you refer to in the tender pack, or don't refer to it. If you want 4D, say so plainly, and say what you'll use it for.

For the audit What the auditor will ask
  • Show me how you found the client's information requirements at tender, including the documents they referred to.
  • Where did you raise gaps, and what assumptions went into the bid?
For the audit What good evidence looks like
  • A requirements list, each line traced to where it came from.
  • A log of the questions asked and the client's answers.
  • Assumptions and exclusions stated in the bid.
From the standard
ISO 19650 What the client should give you at tender

ISO 19650 expects the client to send a tender pack that tells bidders what information they need and how it must be produced. In practice it's spread across the documents, so you go looking.

Look forIn plain words
The information requirementsWhat they need from us, when, and what they'll use it for
Their information standardHow things must be named, classified and formatted
Their methods and proceduresHow information must be made, checked and shared
Reference informationExisting surveys, records and anything else they're giving us to work from
Key datesWhen information is due: the delivery milestones
Security requirementsIf the asset is sensitive, how information must be protected. If there are some, the bid has to answer them
The information protocolThe contract terms about information: who owns it, who can use it, and for what
How the bid will be scoredWhat the client will mark you on, so you know where the effort goes
Anything they refer toOther standards and documents. If it's referred to and you haven't got it, ask

From the ISO 19650 series. ISO 19650 is © ISO and BSI. InfoTown explains the ideas in its own words, for learning; you'll hear the same language across the industry. Read more for free in the Information Management Initiative (IMI) Framework guidance from BSI and nima. For the full requirements, use the standard itself.
