The scene

Every district has its buildings

Each district runs on its own buildings: the systems that part of the business works in. Engineering has the Design System, Commercial has the Commercial System, and so on. One building, the Records Office, sits in Information Governance and serves every district. Every building keeps information, but each one does a different job. Some are built to last. Some are built for one project and taken down at the end. And some information never reaches a building at all: it stays in someone's bag.

If you're the client

List the systems you require in the tender, what each is for, and which ones bidders can't swap for their own.

For the audit What the auditor will ask
  • Which systems are used on this job, who decided, and how does information move between them?
  • How did the bid account for the client's systems?
For the audit What good evidence looks like
  • A one-page systems map from the tender, kept up to date.
  • Licences and training in the price.
  • Routes between systems, each with an owner.
From the standard
ISO 19650 Systems in the tender response

ISO 19650 expects the bid to show the team has the technology to do the job, and how the shared system and its workflows will work. That includes the client's systems, not just ours.

The bid should showIn plain words
The IT the team will useEvery system, ours and theirs, and what each is for
How the shared system will workWhich one is the master, and how things move through it
How systems connectThe routes between them, and who looks after each one
What it costsLicences, set-up and training, priced

From the ISO 19650 series. ISO 19650 is © ISO and BSI. InfoTown explains the ideas in its own words, for learning; you'll hear the same language across the industry.
