The right way, explained

The right way, explained

The plan is our town's promise to the client's town: these roads, these rules, these deliveries. Copy the last town's promise and you've promised roads that don't exist.

If you're the client

If you're the client

Read the plan at tender and score it against your requirements. If you can't tell it apart from the last bid you got, neither can they.

For the audit What the auditor will ask
  • Show me how the plan answers each of the client's requirements.
  • How was it reviewed before it went in?
For the audit What good evidence looks like
  • The plan, mapped to the requirements list.
  • A record of the review, by someone who didn't write it.
  • No ghosts of other projects.
From the standard
ISO 19650 The plan that goes with the bid

ISO 19650 calls it the pre-appointment BIM execution plan. In plain terms, it's how we propose to manage information on this job, written for this client.

It should coverIn plain words
WhoThe named people from the last lesson
HowOur approach to each of the client's requirements
With whatThe systems, from Lesson 2
To what standardTheir standard, and anything we propose to add or change
What we'll deliver, roughly whenA first, high-level delivery plan

From the ISO 19650 series. ISO 19650 is © ISO and BSI. InfoTown explains the ideas in its own words, for learning; you'll hear the same language across the industry. Read more for free in the Information Management Initiative (IMI) Framework guidance from BSI and nima. For the full requirements, use the standard itself.
