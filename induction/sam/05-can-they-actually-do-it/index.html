Think like Sam

A tick isn't evidence. For each firm, what have you actually seen: their logins, their naming, a sample drawing? And not just the shiny models: who plans the work? That's the bit that fails quietly. Where you've seen nothing, what's the plan?

If you're the client

Ask how capability was assessed across the whole team, not just the lead firm. The weakest link sets the pace.

In the town See it in the town: three team photos, and only one of them is honest.

Sam's view: a team photo for every firm. Look for the gaps, and believe the firms that show you theirs.

  1. 1 Each person carries what they can do (capability), the headcount shows whether there are enough of them (capacity), and the kit on the table shows the IT. The honest firm pins its gap on the photo, with a plan to close it.
  2. 2 All ticks and no gaps. Either they've done this exact job before, or nobody looked properly.
  3. 3 Day one: the honest firm's gap is filled. Half the polished firm's team is on the next bid. Check the photo again before any real work starts.

This is the Capability and Capacity Assessment!

Can each firm do it, has it got the people and the kit, and is it still the same team on day one?

For the audit What the auditor will ask
  • How did you assess each firm's capability and capacity?
  • What did you do about the gaps you found?
For the audit What good evidence looks like
  • An assessment for each firm, with evidence behind it.
  • One summary for the whole team.
  • Actions on the gaps, with training records.
From the standard
ISO 19650 Capability and capacity

ISO 19650 expects each task team to assess its own capability and capacity, and the lead to bring the results together into one picture of the whole delivery team.

For each firmIn plain words
CapabilityCan they do it: the skills, the know-how, the systems?
CapacityDo they have enough people and time to do it on this job?
The team summaryOne view across every firm, with the gaps showing
The gapsWhat will be done about each one, by whom and when
