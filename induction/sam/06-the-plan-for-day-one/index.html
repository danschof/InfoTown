In the town See it in the town: what's ready on day one, and who got it ready?

Sam's view: the Site Office, the lines and the signposts, all planned at tender and built before anyone turns up.

Now mobilise it: build and connect the site

In the middle sits the Site Office: the project's CDE, joined to your own Records Office by the home line. Lines run out to every town: the design line to the designers, the client line to the client, the home line back to your own Records Office, the feeder line to the site apps and footpaths to the cabins. The workflows running along all these lines are how one system passes work to another. Signposts tell everyone where each kind of work goes, and the gate decides who gets in.

  1. Build the Site Office
  2. Connect the towns
  3. Plug in the site apps
  4. Put up the signposts
  5. Set up the gate
  6. Test everything

The towns are there. The project site is an empty plot. Your job: mobilise it.

Design line. To and from the designers.
Home line. Back to your own Records Office.
Client line. To and from the Client Town.
Feeder line. What's captured on site must reach the Site Office: by hand, on a schedule, through a link between systems, or because the app is part of the CDE.

From the standard
ISO 19650 The mobilisation plan

ISO 19650 expects the bid to say how the team will get ready: setting up and testing the systems and ways of working before production starts, and the people who'll be trained to use them.

It coversIn plain words
SystemsSet up, connected and tested
Ways of workingThe methods and procedures, tested on something that isn't live
PeopleTrained, with access on day one
TimingDates that fit the programme, with time to test

Based on the ISO 19650 series: the ideas come from the standard; the plain-English wording, examples and advice are InfoTown's. ISO 19650 is © ISO and BSI. You'll hear the same language across the industry. Read more for free in the Information Management Initiative (IMI) Framework guidance from BSI and nima.
