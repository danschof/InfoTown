The right way, explained

The recipe for the town: what's being made, by whom, and when it has to be on the table. A recipe written at tender and never opened is a menu, not a plan.

If you're the project manager The master delivery plan should sit next to your programme.

The master delivery plan should sit next to your programme. If one moves and the other doesn't, ask why.

For the audit What the auditor will ask
  • Show me the master delivery plan, and how it lines up with the programme.
  • Show me who's responsible for one item, and how you know.
For the audit What good evidence looks like
  • Each team's delivery plan.
  • The master plan with dates.
  • The responsibility matrix.
  • Updates over time.

From the standard
ISO 19650 Delivery plans and who does what

ISO 19650 expects each team to plan what information it will produce and when, and the lead firm to roll those into one master plan, alongside a detailed matrix of who is responsible for what.

ExpectIn plain words
Team delivery plansEach team's list: what, when, who
The master delivery planAll of them together, lined up with the programme
The responsibility matrixWho makes, checks and approves each piece
Kept liveUpdated whenever the programme or the scope moves

From the ISO 19650 series. ISO 19650 is © ISO and BSI. InfoTown explains the ideas in its own words, for learning; you'll hear the same language across the industry. Read more for free in the Information Management Initiative (IMI) Framework guidance from BSI and nima. For the full requirements, use the standard itself.
