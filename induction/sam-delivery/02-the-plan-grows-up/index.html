The right way, explained

The right way, explained

The bid's plan is the town's planning application. Once it's granted, it's the permission: you build what was agreed, and changes go back to the planning office. How the builders actually work, day to day, is in the rulebook, and the rulebook gets better every week.

If you're the project manager

If you're the project manager

Know what we promised, because you'll be held to it, and changing it means asking the client first. For how things are done day to day, point people at the rulebook, not the plan.

  • Every change to it, agreed before it was made.
  • The project standard and procedures, issued to every team.

Where Rex comes in: Lesson 9: The Checkpoints

From the standard
ISO 19650 Confirming the plan

After award, ISO 19650 expects the plan from the bid to be confirmed with the real team and agreed with the client, so it can form part of the appointment. Changing it after that is a change to what was agreed. The step-by-step how sits in the project's information standard and its production methods and procedures, which can be improved as the job goes.
