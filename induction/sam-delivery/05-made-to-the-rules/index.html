The right way, explained

The right way, explained

Every firm in town builds to the same street plan, with the same house numbers. Bring your own street plan and your house ends up in somebody's field.

If you're the project manager Ask early whether everyone is working to the same coordinates and templates.

Ask early whether everyone is working to the same coordinates and templates. It's a five-minute question that saves a re-model.

For the audit What the auditor will ask
  • Show me the shared resources you gave each team.
  • How do you know a model was made to the project's standard?
For the audit What good evidence looks like
  • Shared templates and coordinates issued, with a record of who got them.
  • Spot checks of naming and labelling.
  • The level of detail for each item, set out in the plan.

From the standard
ISO 19650 Producing to the rules

ISO 19650 expects every team to check it has the shared resources before it starts, then produce information to the project's standard, methods and level of information need, coordinated with the other teams.

ExpectIn plain words
Shared resourcesTemplates, title blocks, coordinates and libraries, issued to everyone
The information standardNaming, labelling and formats, the project's way
Level of information needHow much detail each thing needs, and no more
CoordinationTeams working from each other's current, shared information

From the ISO 19650 series. ISO 19650 is © ISO and BSI. InfoTown explains the ideas in its own words, for learning; you'll hear the same language across the industry. Read more for free in the Information Management Initiative (IMI) Framework guidance from BSI and nima. For the full requirements, use the standard itself.
