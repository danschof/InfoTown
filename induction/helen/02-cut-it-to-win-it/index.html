The right way, explained

The right way, explained

Information management is the town's roads, and document control is its post office. Close them both and the traffic still has to move. It just goes down the back alleys, slower, and someone ends up paying for the crash. Usually you.

If you're the information manager

If you're the information manager

Put your role in the bid with days a week against it, like the scaffolding. If you're "covering" five jobs, tell Helen before the client finds out.

In the town See it in the town: who keeps the routes open, and what happens when they're cut?

Helen's view: cut the Transport Authority and the routes close. The traffic doesn't stop. It finds the back alleys.

  1. 1 The proper routes between buildings, closed. Nobody is planning them, building them or keeping them open.
  2. 2 The traffic doesn't stop. It squeezes down the back alleys, and nothing it carries leaves a trail.
  3. 3 The Transport Authority: the people who plan the routes, build them, open them to traffic and keep an eye on them. Cut to win the bid.

Sam's Heads Up Spread across five jobs is not a role

One information manager "covering" five projects is a rumour, not a role. Somebody has to be on the job day to day, or the shared place turns back into email within a month.

Before the bid goes in, check who is named for information on this job, and how many days a week they're actually on it.

The briefing Four lines for the board
The point

Information management and document control are part of delivering the contract, not overhead to trim.

Why it matters

Cutting the roles doesn't remove the work. It spreads it across engineers paid to do something else, and leaves nobody accountable.

What the standard expects

When a client asks for ISO 19650, the bid has to show who will manage information and that the team can actually do it.

Your decision

Keep the information roles in the price, or accept the risk in writing.

From the standard
For directors What a tender has to show

When a client asks for ISO 19650, the tender response is expected to show more than a price. In plain terms, the bid should show:

The bid showsIn plain words
A plan for managing informationHow we'll run information on this job (the pre-appointment BEP)
That the team can do itPeople, skills and IT, checked for every firm in the team
A plan for day oneHow the shared place, rules and training will be ready before work starts
The risksWhat could go wrong with information, and what we'll do about it

For you: if the information roles aren't in the price, the bid is promising things nobody is paid to deliver.
