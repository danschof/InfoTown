"That's IM's problem"

On site

Site pours the base slab for the pumping station's wet well. Lovely job. Dead level.

The site cabin

They poured it from revision B, printed in the cabin in May. Revision D moved the inlet to the other side. It's in someone's email.

Who did this?

Project director: That's IM's problem.

Who did this, then?

Sam: I set up the system and the rules. The project decided not to use them.

Project director: "I was busy building a pumping station."

Round and round

Everyone points at someone else, in a neat circle. Helen is in the middle. The concrete has set.

Phone: CLIENT

Client: Why is our pumping station facing the wrong way?

The right way: Owned by the project, joined up by Sam

The project

The project owns its information, the same way it owns its safety. The project director makes sure the team follows the rules.

Sam

Sam keeps everything joined up: the rules, the shared place for files, the signposts and the training.

Day one

Who does what is named and written down at the start, so "that's IM's problem" isn't an answer anyone gets away with.

Monthly report

When something goes wrong, the project director fixes it and tells Sam what broke. Helen reads about it in the report, not on her phone.

The right way, explained

The right way, explained

Sam is the town's planning department. Planning draws the roads and puts up the signs. It doesn't drive every car, and it can't stop people parking on the pavement.

Ask this

Who on this project owns its information? Not IM. Who on the project?

The briefing Four lines for the board
The point

The project owns its information. The information manager sets things up and keeps them joined up.

Why it matters

If it's "IM's problem", nobody on the project fixes it, and the client finds out first.

What the standard expects

ISO 19650 lets you hand the information management work to a specialist, but the responsibility stays with the organisation that signed the contract.

Your decision

Make information a named responsibility of every project leader, and check it the way you check safety.
