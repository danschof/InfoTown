The right way, explained

The right way, explained

Sam is the town's planning department. Planning draws the roads and puts up the signs. It doesn't drive every car, and it can't stop people parking on the pavement.

Sam's Heads Up I can't stop people parking on the pavement

I can set up the shared place, write the rules and put the signs up. I can't make a site team use them. That takes the project director, every week, like safety.

Ask your project directors one thing: who on your job owns its information? If the answer is "Sam", we've got a problem.

The briefing Four lines for the board
The point

The project owns its information. The information manager sets things up and keeps them joined up.

Why it matters

If it's "IM's problem", nobody on the project fixes it, and the client finds out first.

What the standard expects

ISO 19650 lets you hand the information management work to a specialist, but the responsibility stays with the organisation that signed the contract.

Your decision

Make information a named responsibility of every project leader, and check it the way you check safety.

From the standard
For directors You can hand over the work, not the responsibility

ISO 19650 lets an organisation hand its information management tasks to someone else: a specialist, a consultant, an in-house team. It doesn't let it hand over the responsibility.

The standard also expects everyone's information duties to be written down, firm by firm and person by person, so nobody can say they didn't know.
