The right way, explained

The right way, explained

A contract is the town's promise to its neighbours. If the Town Hall signs a promise it hasn't read, the town keeps it by accident or not at all. And a written plan cuts both ways: it's also how you show the neighbours were late.

The briefing Four lines for the board
The point

The contract makes information promises. Know what they are before you sign, and have the plans in place from day one.

Why it matters

The plan is your evidence. Without one, you can't show the client was late too, and you carry the blame alone.

What the standard expects

Under ISO 19650, the information requirements, the plan and the delivery schedule go into the appointment, and down to every subcontractor.

Your decision

Don't sign until someone can tell you, on one page, what information you're promising.

From the standard
For directors What goes into the contract

Under ISO 19650, the information side of the job isn't a nice-to-have. It's written into the appointment, the contract between the client and the lead firm, and the same again in every appointment below it.

In the contractWhat it means for you
The client's information requirementsWhat they need from us, and when
Their information standard and methodsHow files must be named, checked and shared
Our plan for managing informationWhat we promised in the bid, now binding
The delivery planWhich drawing, report or model is due when, and who makes it

For you: the delivery plan is also your defence. If the client's reviews run late, the plan is how you show it.
