The right way, explained

The Town Hall doesn't make information. It runs on whatever the town sends up the road. If it never says what it needs, every district sends something different, and the Town Hall picks whichever arrives first.

From the standard
For directors The organisation's information requirements, in one minute

ISO 19650 calls them the organisational information requirements. Every organisation has its own, and they come from three plain questions: what must we be able to show, what do we keep running, and what do we have to decide?

The client's Town Hall answers them for its assets, and those answers become the order for each job. Ours are different: which jobs are behind, what's costing more than planned, the defects and claims we must answer, and the records we must keep.

Everything else follows from them. The information you ask for about your assets and your projects should trace back to these questions, and each project's requirements should trace back to those.

For you: nobody else can set these. If the board never decides its questions, every project answers a different one.
