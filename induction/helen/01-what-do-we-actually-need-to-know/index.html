The right way, explained

The right way, explained

The Town Hall doesn't make information. It runs on whatever the town sends up the road. If it never says what it needs, every district sends something different, and the Town Hall picks whichever arrives first.

The briefing Four lines for the board
The point

The board's questions should be decided, written down, and answered from the projects' own information.

Why it matters

Three numbers is the same as no number, and the client notices first.

What the standard expects

ISO 19650 starts with the organisation's own information requirements: the questions its leaders need answered. Project requirements should trace back to them.

Your decision

Agree the questions, and who answers each one.

From the standard
For directors The organisation's information requirements, in one minute

ISO 19650 calls them the organisational information requirements. They're the questions the business needs answered to run itself: which jobs are behind, what's costing more than planned, what we're handing over.

Everything else follows from them. The information you ask for about your assets and your projects should trace back to these questions, and each project's requirements should trace back to those.

For you: nobody else can set these. If the board never decides its questions, every project answers a different one.
