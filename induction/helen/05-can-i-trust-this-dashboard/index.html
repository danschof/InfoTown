A feeling, not a forecast

Quarterly review

The dashboard says the pumping station is on budget. Lovely green bar.

The board

Helen tells the board. The board is pleased.

Behind the bar

A spreadsheet on someone's desktop, last updated before a big design change.

Meanwhile

The real number is in an email nobody forwarded.

Two weeks later

The bar turns red overnight. It was a feeling, not a forecast.

Phone: CEO

CEO: The board would like a word.

The right way: Numbers that came the proper route

The dashboard

It pulls from information that came the proper route: from the shared place, checked, with a date and an owner.

Where from?

When a number matters, Helen asks "where did this come from?" and gets a one-sentence answer.

Design change

When the design changes, the forecast changes with it, because the change travelled the same route.

Early warning

The bad news still comes, but early, while there's time to do something about it.

The right way, explained

The right way, explained

The Town Hall sees the town through a window. If someone has hung a painting of last spring over the window, it's still a lovely view. It just isn't the town.

The briefing Four lines for the board
The point

Board numbers should come from checked, shared project information, not private spreadsheets.

Why it matters

A decision is only as good as its source, and late bad news is the expensive kind.

What the standard expects

ISO 19650 expects information to be checked and shared in one common place before anyone else relies on it.

Your decision

Keep asking where the key numbers come from, until the answer is boring.
