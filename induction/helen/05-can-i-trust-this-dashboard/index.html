The briefing Four lines for the board
The point

Board numbers should come from checked, shared project information, not private spreadsheets.

Why it matters

A decision is only as good as its source, and late bad news is the expensive kind.

What the standard expects

ISO 19650 expects information to be checked and shared in one common place before anyone else relies on it.

Your decision

Keep asking where the key numbers come from, until the answer is boring.

From the standard
For directors Which information you can trust

ISO 19650 describes one shared place for a project's information, called the common data environment (CDE). Information moves through it in stages, and each stage tells you how far you can trust it.

StageWhat it meansDecide from it?
Work in progressStill being made, by one teamNo
SharedChecked and shared with the rest of the teamFor coordination
PublishedAuthorised and accepted for useYes
ArchivedThe record of what was used, and whenFor the audit trail

For you: a number from someone's desktop isn't at any of these stages. That's the problem.

From the ISO 19650 series. ISO 19650 is © ISO and BSI. InfoTown explains the ideas in its own words, for learning; you'll hear the same language across the industry. Read more for free in the Information Management Initiative (IMI) Framework guidance from BSI and nima. For the full requirements, use the standard itself.
