The right way, explained

The right way, explained

An audit is the town's inspection. The inspector doesn't want to see the plans. They want to walk the streets and say "show me", and the people who live on those streets should be the ones showing them round, not just the planning department.

If you're the information manager

If you're the information manager

Get the team in the room, and keep the evidence findable every week, not the week before. Then tell Helen the audit was boring. That's the good news.

Sam's Heads Up Audits aren't scary. Empty rooms are.

The assessor doesn't want a slideshow from me. They want to talk to the people who did the work and see the evidence where it lives.

Six weeks out, I need three things from a director: people in the room, evidence found before the day, and someone senior saying out loud that this matters.

The briefing Four lines for the board
The point

An audit tests the projects' information, not the IM team. The project teams have to be there.

Why it matters

Some clients ask for ISO 19650 certification before they'll let you bid. For them: no certificate, no bid.

How it works

Certification is run by independent certification bodies, not by the standard itself. They judge you on evidence from real projects, so the assessor wants the people who did the work.

Your decision

Back your information manager. Make the audit a project priority, and own the actions afterwards.

For directors Certification, in plain words

Organisations can be independently certified as working to ISO 19650. An assessor from a certification body checks how you manage information, then samples real projects to see if it happens.

Some clients ask for that certificate before they'll let you bid.
