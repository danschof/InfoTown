Think like Priya

Every inspection is a piece of information you owe someone. In week one, ask: which inspections, who does them, how much notice, and where does the record go? If it isn't planned, it won't happen. If it isn't written down, it didn't.

The right way, explained

The right way, explained

Some roads in town have a toll booth you can't drive round. A hold point is a toll booth: it goes on the map before anyone sets off, not after they've driven through it.

If you're the site manager

If you're the site manager

Book inspections the way you book concrete: with a date, a name and notice. Nothing pours until the record exists. "It looked fine" is not a record.

The scene

"What are we building?"

Every project starts with the client's question. The design team turns it into a list of drawings to make. The site team turns it into details, questions and inspections. Only then does anybody start making things, inside the right building.

Inspection records are information too!

Each one is owed to someone, planned in week one, and on the parts list like any drawing.

Sam's Heads Up It might not be called an inspection plan

The standard way: Plan what you owe, with an owner and a date.

On your job: Your job might call it an inspection and test plan, a quality plan or a hold-point schedule. Whatever it's called, it sits next to the delivery plan, not in a drawer.

For the audit What the auditor will ask
  • Show me the inspection plan, and where the hold points sit in the programme.
  • Show me the record for this pour.
For the audit What good evidence looks like
  • An inspection plan with hold points, owners and notice periods.
  • A signed record for each hold point, against the right drawing revision, dated before the pour.

From the standard
ISO 19650 Planning what's delivered

ISO 19650 expects the information a team has to deliver to be planned up front, each piece with an owner and a date. That inspection records belong on that plan is InfoTown's advice; how you run inspections is your quality system, not ISO 19650.

Based on the ISO 19650 series: the ideas come from the standard; the plain-English wording, examples and advice are InfoTown's. ISO 19650 is © ISO and BSI.
