Think like Priya

Documents tell people how to work; records prove what happened. A document has to be the current one every time someone uses it. When this one changes, how does the old copy disappear, and how will you know who was briefed on what?

The scene

Goods In at the Site Office

Every parcel on the project passes through Goods In at the Site Office, the project's CDE. The sorters never open the boxes. They read the label and send each parcel down the right line. A parcel with no label can't go anywhere, so it ends up in the in-tray, where nobody will ever look again.

The standard way: Work from the current version on the CDE.

On your job: Sites need printed copies, and that's fine. Put the revision on the front in big letters, date it, and make swapping them someone's named job.

For the audit What the auditor will ask
  • Show me the current method statement for this work, and the briefing record that goes with it.
  • How do old revisions get withdrawn on site?
For the audit What good evidence looks like
  • Method statements on the CDE with their revisions and status.
  • Briefing records that name the revision used and who attended.
  • A note of superseded copies withdrawn, and when.

From the standard
ISO 19650 Working from the current version

Controlling method statements and procedures is your management system's job. ISO 19650's part is the rule it applies to drawings too: work from the current, shared version, and always know which revision is which.
