The right way

That morning

Site spots the wrong opening and stops. The defect is logged that morning: what, where, with photos.

Agreed first

The fix is agreed with the designer in writing before anyone picks up a drill.

Done and drawn

The fix is done and inspected, and the drawing is revised to show it.

Closed

The log is closed with its evidence: the agreement, the inspection and the new revision. At final inspection the Clerk of Works reads it and moves on.

"Sorted"A patch nobody can explain, and a certificate held for a fortnight.
Logged, agreed, closedA fix with its evidence, and a drawing that matches.
Think like Priya

The fix is only half the job. Who agreed it, what changed, and does the drawing say so now? Close it with evidence, not with "sorted".

If you're the site manager

If you're the site manager

Fix fast, but log first. Ten minutes of photos and a note saves a fortnight of cores. And nothing permanent changes without the designer saying yes, in writing.

In the town See it in the town: fixed by lunch. So why the fortnight?

Priya's view: a fix is a new parcel. It goes back through the checkpoints like any other before the map is redrawn.

Not working in the CDE. Your team works in its own system, and the four checkpoints happen there. Approved, the parcel crosses the hoarding into the CDE, and Goods In checks it at the gate: is it labelled right, in the right format, going to the right place? Only then does it reach the shared table. Your project decides which way it works. Either way, the four checkpoints still happen.

Sam's Heads Up Not every fix needs the designer

InfoTown good practice: A change to the design goes back through the checkpoints.

On your job: Some fixes are like for like and change nothing on the drawing. Fine, as long as someone decides that, and writes down who.

For the audit What the auditor will ask
  • Show me your open and closed non-conformances, and the evidence behind one fix.
  • How do you make sure the drawings change when the work does?
For the audit What good evidence looks like
  • A log of each defect: photos, the cause, the designer's agreement, the inspection, the revised drawing and the date it closed.
  • Revised drawings that match the fixes in the log.

Where Rex comes in: Rex's Lesson 9, The Checkpoints: the revised drawing goes back through them like any other. Stevie's Drawn versus built shows where an unrecorded change bites later.

From the standard
ISO 19650 When the design changes

Non-conformance reports belong to your quality system, not to ISO 19650. ISO 19650's part is the change: a revised design travels the same route as any other information, checked and shared through the CDE.
