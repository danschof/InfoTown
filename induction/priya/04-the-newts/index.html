The right way, explained

The right way, explained

Some parts of town have rules you can't see from the road: a protected garden, a bridge you can only cross in summer. The town map marks them, or the next builder drives straight through.

If you're the site manager

If you're the site manager

Ask for the constraints plan before anything is stripped or dug, and make the no-go areas part of every induction and every permit to dig. If it isn't on the plan, ask before you assume it's fine.

In the town See it in the town: the newts were in the report. So why was the digger in the pond?

Priya's view: a warning sign on the road ahead for every rule you can't see from the road, each with someone's name on it.

  1. 1 A warning sign for every information risk on the road ahead, written like any other project risk, each with a named owner and something being done about it.
  2. 2 The old register's only sign. Faded, vague, and nobody's name on it.

Legal constraints are requirements too!

A licence, a permit or a season is a requirement like any other, and the information about it has to reach the digger: listed, owned, mapped and briefed.

Sam's Heads Up Your newts might be bats

InfoTown good practice: Legal constraints are requirements like any other; they go on the list and reach the work.

On your job: It might be bats in the old station's roof, birds nesting in the hedge, or a limit on silty water going into the river. A different creature, the same route.

For the audit What the auditor will ask
  • Show me the environmental constraints for this site, and how the site team knows them.
  • Show me how a licence condition got into the programme.
For the audit What good evidence looks like
  • A register of licences, permits and their conditions, with owners and dates.
  • A constraints plan on the CDE that matches it.
  • Induction records that cover the no-go areas.

From the standard
ISO 19650 Requirements that reach the work

ISO 19650 expects requirements to be captured and passed down to the people doing the work. It doesn't name legal or environmental obligations; treating them as requirements that must reach the work face is InfoTown's advice.

Based on the ISO 19650 series: the ideas come from the standard; the plain-English wording, examples and advice are InfoTown's. ISO 19650 is © ISO and BSI.
