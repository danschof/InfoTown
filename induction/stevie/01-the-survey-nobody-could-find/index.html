Think like Stevie

When you make something other people will build on, ask where they'll look for it. If the answer is "my laptop", they won't find it. Every survey needs a date, a datum and an address the whole team can reach.

The right way, explained

The town's base map lives in the library, not in the surveyor's coat pocket. Anyone drawing a new street starts from the library copy, and the library knows which copy is the newest.

If you're the designer

If you're the designer

Before you draw a line, check the survey's date and datum. If the newest survey is older than the last big change on site, say so and ask for a new one.

Stevie's view: the survey belongs in the Records Office, where every building can borrow it, not in one surveyor's drawer.

The scene

Every district has its buildings

Each district runs on its own buildings: the systems that part of the business works in. Engineering has the Design System, Commercial has the Commercial System, and so on. One building, the Records Office, sits in Information Governance and serves every district. Every building keeps information, but each one does a different job. Some are built to last. Some are built for one project and taken down at the end. And some information never reaches a building at all: it stays in someone's bag.

Watch out for

This is Reference Information!

What everyone builds from: surveys, records and anything else that was there before us, shared and never edited.

Sam's Heads Up The newest survey may not be yours

The standard way: Existing information is gathered and shared before anyone designs.

On your job: The latest survey might sit with the client or a previous contractor. Ask for it in writing, and record what you were given, and when.

For the audit What the auditor will ask
  • Which survey was this design based on, and how did the designer know it was the latest?
  • How do you keep reference information apart from work in progress?
For the audit What good evidence looks like
  • The survey on the CDE, with its date, datum and status.
  • The design recording which survey it used.

From the standard
ISO 19650 Reference information

ISO 19650 expects the existing information a team works from, like surveys, to be gathered and made available to everyone before production starts.

Based on the ISO 19650 series: the ideas come from the standard; the plain-English wording, examples and advice are InfoTown's. ISO 19650 is © ISO and BSI.
