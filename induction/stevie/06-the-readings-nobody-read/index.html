Before digging

The designer's trigger levels go into a plan: green, amber and red, who is told at each, how fast, and what happens next.

Every morning

Readings go into one shared place the same morning, where the site manager and the designer can both see them.

Tuesday, amber

Stevie rings the site manager and the designer before lunch. Digging pauses, the designer checks, and extra props go in.

Wednesday

The readings settle. No crack, and a short note on the record says what happened and why.

Think like Stevie

Information someone has to act on needs a deadline as well as an address. Who needs to see this reading, how quickly, and what do they do when it's amber? If the answer is "it's in Friday's PDF", it's already late.

Sam's Heads Up Sensors don't read their own texts

InfoTown good practice: Monitoring data is shared, acted on and kept.

On your job: Automatic sensors can text people when a trigger trips. Check who's actually on the list, and test it before the dig starts.

For the audit What the auditor will ask
  • Show me the trigger levels, and who is told when one is reached.
  • Show me what happened the last time a reading went amber.
For the audit What good evidence looks like
  • A monitoring plan with trigger levels, actions and named contacts.
  • Readings shared the same day they're taken.
  • A record of each trigger and the response to it.

From the standard
ISO 19650 Monitoring data

ISO 19650 doesn't set trigger levels or alert times; those come from the designer and your project. What it adds is that monitoring data is project information like any other: planned, shared through the CDE and kept.

Based on the ISO 19650 series: the ideas come from the standard; the plain-English wording, examples and advice are InfoTown's. ISO 19650 is © ISO and BSI.
