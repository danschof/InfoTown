Stevie's view: a 400GB scan is still a parcel. It needs a label and a known address, even if it lives in the warehouse.

The scene

People come and go. The work travels as parcels.

People move between buildings to do their work, as long as they have the right access. But the work itself travels packed into parcels, so it can go where people can't, and arrive in one piece. Drawings, models, questions, photos and registers: each one is its own parcel. Several parcels sent together make a shipment. Pick a parcel to see what's inside.

Your own room, then the shared table

Each team works in its own room until its parcel is ready for others to build on. Then it goes on the shared table in the CDE, so the drainage team draws round the beams that actually exist, not the ones from three weeks ago. Clashes get spotted on the table, not on site.

A point cloud is an Information Container too!

Big or small, a scan, a model or a drawing: if it has a name and someone needs it, it's a parcel to be managed.

Sam's Heads Up "Too big" is a storage problem, not an excuse

The standard way: What the team shares is managed through the CDE.

On your job: Your CDE may choke on scans and models. That's fine. Agree a proper store for them, and keep the record, the status and the link in the CDE.

For the audit What the auditor will ask
  • Where is the survey of the existing structure, and who can get to it?
  • What happens if that drive or server fails?
For the audit What good evidence looks like
  • An agreed place for large data, written into the plan.
  • A CDE record for each scan, with its date, coordinates and accuracy.
  • A backup, tested at least once.

From the standard
ISO 19650 Information containers

ISO 19650 counts any named set of information you can retrieve from a file, system or application as an information container, so a point cloud is one. It expects containers to be managed through the CDE, which can be more than one system.

Based on the ISO 19650 series: the ideas come from the standard; the plain-English wording, examples and advice are InfoTown's. ISO 19650 is © ISO and BSI. You'll hear the same language across the industry. Read more for free in the Information Management Initiative (IMI) Framework guidance from BSI and nima.
