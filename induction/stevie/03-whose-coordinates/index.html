The scene

A shared place, just for this job

In Lesson 3 you met the firms on a job: the client, the firm leading the work, and the firms doing it. Each has its own town, and nobody moves into anyone else's. Instead, they set up one shared place for this job: the project site. Nobody owns it, everybody works through it, and it's taken down when the job ends. Before anyone can use it, it has to be built, connected and signposted. That's mobilisation.

Now mobilise it: build and connect the site

In the middle sits the Site Office: the project's CDE, an extension of your own Records Office. Lines run out to every town: the design line to the designers, the client line to the client, the home line back to your own Records Office, the feeder line to the site apps and footpaths to the cabins. The workflows running along all these lines are how one system passes work to another. Signposts tell everyone where each kind of work goes, and the gate decides who gets in.

The towns are there. The project site is an empty plot. Your job: mobilise it.

These are Shared Resources!

The same kit for every firm, handed out before anyone starts: templates, title blocks, and the job's one set of coordinates.

Sam's Heads Up Your job might have three grids

The standard way: One coordinate system for the job, issued to everyone.

On your job: There may be a national grid, a site grid and a structural grid. That's fine, as long as someone has written down which one wins and how the others tie to it.

For the audit What the auditor will ask
  • Which coordinate system and survey control does this job use, and how did each firm get them?
  • How do you know a new firm's setting out matches yours?
For the audit What good evidence looks like
  • The survey control in the shared kit, with a revision and a date.
  • A record of each firm checking into the control before it started.
