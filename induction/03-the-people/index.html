The right way, explained

The right way, explained

Every job has the same cast: the client it's for, the firm leading the work, and the firms doing it, each split into small teams. Rex's drawing went nowhere because nobody had written down who makes it, who checks it and who says yes. One page fixed it.

In the town See it in the town: who's it for, who's leading, and who's doing the work?
The scene

Who works for whom

Every project town is built the same way. In the middle is the client, who the work is for. Around them are the firms leading the work, each with its own appointment. Each lead firm brings its own firms to do the work, and inside every firm are small teams doing a task. Your firm and the firms working for it make up your delivery team. Everyone together is the project team. Pick a group to see who's in it.

Appointed by the client, and answerable for its part of the job. Rex works for one.

Watch out for

These are Appointing Parties, Appointed Parties and Task Teams!

ISO 19650 gives the groups of people in the town names, so everyone knows who works for whom.

The client is the appointing party. The organisation leading the work for them is the lead appointed party. The teams doing the work are task teams. Who makes, checks and approves each piece of information is written down in a responsibility matrix: your who-does-what list.

Sam's Heads Up One firm, two hats

The firm that leads the job can make some of the parcels too. Rex's firm does both: it leads the whole team, and its own engineers produce work like everyone else's.

And not every appointment is a contract between two companies. Inside one organisation it can simply be an instruction to a team. Either way, write down which hat each team is wearing.

Fix it Not sure who? Find out first.

Not sure who?
Find out first.

  1. Ask who checks it before you start, not after you finish.
  2. Write the answer down.
  3. Put it where the next new starter will find it.

A note for the Coordinators (that's IM): write down who makes, checks and says yes for each kind of work. One page is plenty, as long as people can actually find it!

More detail
The people test For every kind of work, can you name…

Who makes it?

Written downA named role, so the work has an owner.
Not written downWhoever happened to pick it up.

Who checks it?

Written downA named checker who knows it's their job.
Not written downEveryone thinks someone else has.

Who says yes?

Written downOne person with the authority to approve it.
Not written downWhoever was last to see it, by default.
The real town Why is it so hard to say who does what?

Because the people in the town don't stand still.

People wear many hats

The same person makes one drawing and checks the next. Sometimes before lunch.

Roles change mid-job

People join, leave and get moved around. The drawing doesn't know.

Titles don't say it

"Senior engineer" tells you nothing about what they check.

The org chart isn't the information chart

Who you report to is rarely who checks your work.

ISO 19650 Who's responsible for what: RACI and the responsibility matrices

ISO 19650 uses three tools to pin down who does what. They get more detailed as the job firms up.

ToolWhat it answersWhenIn the town
Information management assignment matrixWho does each information management task: setting requirements, the CDE, the standards, the checksBefore anyone is appointedWho runs the town
High-level responsibility matrixWhich part of the job each firm in a delivery team is responsible for, and its key deliverablesIn the tender responseWhich firm owns which part
Detailed responsibility matrixWhat information is produced, when it's exchanged and with whom, and which task team produces itAt appointmentThe who-does-what list
Task information delivery plan (TIDP)Each task team's own list of what it will deliver, and whenAt appointment, then kept up to dateEach team's page of the parts list (Lesson 4)

RACI: the letters in the assignment matrix

LetterMeansFor a drainage drawing, for example
RResponsible: does the workAlex's task team in Designer Town
AAccountable: answers for it being done (only one per task)Designer Town, the firm appointed to deliver it
CConsulted: asked before or duringThe structures team, whose beams the drain has to miss
IInformed: told once it's doneDave and the site team

One A per task. If two people are accountable, nobody is.
