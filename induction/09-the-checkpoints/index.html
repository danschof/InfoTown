The right way, explained

The right way, explained

Before work is shared it passes four checkpoints: someone makes it, someone else checks it, someone reviews it, and someone approves it. The rules for each are written down before the job starts, in the rulebook. A signature without a check is just a rubber stamp.

Sam's Heads Up Four checkpoints is the minimum, not the maximum

Your company's check-and-approve process might have six steps, two signatures and a form nobody can find. That's fine, as long as the four here are in there somewhere and someone actually does them.

The rules for your job live in its BEP and your company's procedures. If they say something different from this page, they win. If they say nothing at all, that's a bigger problem.

Fix it Got a rubber stamp problem?

Got a rubber
stamp problem?

  1. Pick one recent drawing and ask who actually checked it, and against what.
  2. Write down your four checkpoints and who owns each one.
  3. Put them in the BEP and the CDE workflow, so the route enforces them.

A note for the Coordinators (that's IM): write the rulebook before work starts, and build the checkpoints into the route so parcels can't skip them. A stamp without a check is just ink!

The reveal

This is an Approval Process!

Originate, check, review, approve: a parcel is only shared once someone with the authority says it's right.

ISO 19650 expects a quality assurance check and a review against the information requirements before information is approved for sharing and moves from work in progress to the shared state. The BIM Execution Plan (BEP) records how the delivery team will do this, and a security-minded approach (ISO 19650-5) decides who gets to see what.

Status codes

CodeStateWhat that means to youBuild from it?
S0Work in progressSomeone is still working on it in their own team's area. You shouldn't normally see it, and you shouldn't rely on it.No
S1SharedShared so other teams can check their work fits around it. Expect it to change.No

Revision codes

CodeWhenWhat that means to you
P01.01, P01.02…Work in progressStill being worked on. Every save gets a new version number. Nobody outside the team should be using it.
P01, P02…SharedA shared draft. The number goes up every time it's shared again, so check you've got the highest one.
C01, C02…PublishedThe signed-off version. Higher is newer. Build from the latest C, with an A status.

An S code says what something may be used for. An A code only says it's been signed off, and the n is the work stage. The Annex ties the stages to BS 8536-1 (buildings) and BS 8536-2 (infrastructure), which follow the familiar Stage 0 to 7 pattern of the RIBA Plan of Work, so on this site a construction issue is A5 and the as-built record is A6, unless your project's information standard sets its own. What each A code is issued for still has to be written in that standard.
