The right way, explained

The right way, explained

Before work is shared it passes four checkpoints: someone makes it, someone else checks it, someone reviews it, and someone approves it. The rules for each are written down before the job starts, in the rulebook. A signature without a check is just a rubber stamp.

In the town See it in the town: four stops before anything is shared. What's the whole thing called?
The scene

Four checkpoints on the road out

Between the Design System and the shared table sit four checkpoints. The maker finishes it, someone else checks it's made properly, someone reviews whether it does the job, and someone with the authority approves it for sharing. Above the road hangs the rulebook that says how. Pick a checkpoint to see what happens there.

Not working in the CDE. Your team works in its own system, and the four checkpoints happen there. Only then does it reach the shared table. Your project decides which way it works. Either way, the four checkpoints still happen.

Watch out for

This is an Approval Process!

Originate, check, review, approve: a parcel is only shared once someone with the authority says it's right.

ISO 19650 expects a quality assurance check and a review against the information requirements before information is approved for sharing and moves from work in progress to the shared state. The project's information standard and its production methods and procedures set out how the team does this, and a security-minded approach (ISO 19650-5) decides who gets to see what.

Sam's Heads Up Four checkpoints is the minimum, not the maximum

Your company's check-and-approve process might have six steps, two signatures and a form nobody can find. That's fine, as long as the four here are in there somewhere and someone actually does them.

The rules for your job live in its information standard and procedures. The BEP says which way the team promised to work; the procedures say how, step by step. If they say something different from this page, they win. If they say nothing at all, that's a bigger problem.

Sam's Heads Up Being able to click it doesn't mean you're allowed to

Approving, authorising and accepting each commit your firm to something. Only the people your firm has actually given that authority to should do them, and the CDE should only let those people press the button.

Check who on your job can approve. If the answer is "whoever's in a hurry", or someone who passed the job to a colleague along with their login, fix the permissions before the next Friday afternoon.

Fix it Got a rubber stamp problem?

Got a rubber
stamp problem?

  1. Pick one recent drawing and ask who actually checked it, and against what.
  2. Write down your four checkpoints and who owns each one.
  3. Put them in the project's procedures and the CDE workflow, so the route enforces them.

A note for the Coordinators (that's IM): write the rulebook before work starts, and build the checkpoints into the route so parcels can't skip them. A stamp without a check is just ink!

Revision codes

CodeWhenWhat that means to you
P01.01, P01.02…Work in progressStill being worked on. The second number is the version within that revision, so the team can keep track of its working versions before the next shared revision. Nobody outside the team should be using it.
