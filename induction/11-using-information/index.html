The right way, explained

The right way, explained

Information only matters when somebody uses it, and every use runs the same line: need it, find it, trust it, use it, prove it. Dave's van copy was handy but couldn't be trusted. The site app gave him the right revision in thirty seconds, and the proof to go with it.

The reveal

This is Information Use!

Getting the right information to the person who needs it, in a form they can find, trust, use and prove later.

Everything ISO 19650 asks for, from requirements and labels to checks and acceptance, exists so information can be used with confidence. Using it well means working from published, current information and keeping a record of what was used. That record becomes part of the information the client keeps for the life of the asset: the thing you built.

UK National Annex Can I build from it?
If the label says…It meansBuild from it?
S0 with P01.03Still being worked onNo
S1–S3 with P02Shared for coordination, information or commentNo
S4 / S5 with P03Waiting for the lead to authorise, or the client to acceptNot yet
A code with C04Signed off, for the work stage in the codeYes, for that stage, if it's the latest

An S code says what something may be used for. An A code only says it's been signed off, and the n is the work stage. The Annex ties the stages to BS 8536-1 (buildings) and BS 8536-2 (infrastructure), which follow the familiar Stage 0 to 7 pattern of the RIBA Plan of Work, so on this site a construction issue is A5 and the as-built record is A6, unless your project's information standard sets its own. What each A code is issued for still has to be written in that standard.

Based on the UK National Annex to BS EN ISO 19650-2. Your project's information standard can add to it. ISO 19650 and its UK National Annex are © ISO and BSI.
