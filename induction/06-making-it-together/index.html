The right way, explained

The right way, explained

What travels between people is a parcel: one drawing, one model, one report, each on its own. Several can travel together as a shipment, but each one keeps its own identity. When one drawing changed, one parcel went out, and everyone knew exactly what had moved.

Fix it Got a bundle? Unpack it.

Got a bundle?
Unpack it.

  1. Split it back into one parcel per drawing, model or report.
  2. Give each one its own name.
  3. Send them together as one shipment.

A note for the Coordinators (that's IM): if people keep bundling, make shipments easy! One click to send a set of parcels together, each still with its own name.

In the town See it in the town: People come and go. The work travels as parcels.
The scene

People come and go. The work travels as parcels.

People move between buildings to do their work, as long as they have the right access. But the work itself travels packed into parcels, so it can go where people can't, and arrive in one piece. Drawings, models, questions, photos and registers: each one is its own parcel. Several parcels sent together make a shipment. Pick a parcel to see what's inside.

Your own room, then the shared table

Each team works in its own room until its parcel is ready for others to build on. Then it goes on the shared table in the CDE, so the drainage team draws round the beams that actually exist, not the ones from three weeks ago. Clashes get spotted on the table, not on site.

The reveal

This is an Information Container!

Every parcel is an information container: one thing, with its own name, that can be sent, changed and found on its own.

ISO 19650 calls any named, persistent set of information an information container: a drawing, a model, a document or a set of data. Containers travel together in information exchanges, which is what a shipment is. Putting work in progress into a shared state so other teams can build on it is collaborative production, and it's how coordination happens.
