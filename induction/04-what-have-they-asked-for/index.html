The right way, explained

The right way, explained

You already know this one. Your clothes were all over the floor, so you ordered a wardrobe that fits, and it turned up as twelve boxes and a parts list. The pumping station is the same, just bigger: the client places the order, and the teams turn it into a parts list of everything to make, who makes it and when. If it isn't on the parts list, it doesn't get made, or it gets made in a panic. Alex's roof detail was a lovely extra shelf nobody ordered. Priya's inspections only existed because they were on the list in week one.

The scene

"What are we building?"

Every project starts with the client's question. The design team turns it into a list of drawings to make. The site team turns it into details, questions and inspections. Only then does anybody start making things, inside the right building.

Show:
The need, turned into a plan. Who makes what, and by when.

This is the Information Delivery Plan!

The order and the parts list: the client's needs (the information requirements), turned into a list of every part to make, who makes it, how much detail it needs, by when, and whether it goes to the client.

ISO 19650 calls the client's needs information requirements, from the organisation's big picture down to each individual delivery. The list of what each team will make, and when, is the information delivery plan. Information still being made sits in the work in progress state, inside the team's own space.

Check the need.

  1. Ask what the client actually needs from you.
  2. Write the list: what, who, by when.
  3. Add the inspections and records you'll need to prove it.

A note for the Coordinators (that's IM): start every project with the client's question: what are we building, and what do we need to prove it? Then turn the answers into a list everyone can actually see!

The need test Before you make anything, can you answer…

Who needs it?

Written downA named person or team is waiting for it.
In someone's head"I just assumed someone would want it."

What for?

Written downIt answers a question or lets someone do a job.
In someone's head"It seemed like a good idea at the time."

By when?

Written downA date that fits the work that depends on it.
In someone's head"ASAP." So, never, then everything at once.

ISO 19650 saysMeansIn the town
Organisational information requirements (OIR)What the client's organisation needs to know to run itselfThe client's big picture
Asset information requirements (AIR)What they need to know to run the assetWhat the drain needs for the next forty years
Project information requirements (PIR)What they need to know to make decisions on this project"What are we building?"
Exchange information requirements (EIR)Exactly what each appointed party has to deliver, and to what standardWhat goes to the client, and when
Information delivery milestonesWhen information has to be deliveredThe delivery dates on the order
Level of information needHow much detail is enough, and no more"How much detail does this ingredient need?"
Task information delivery plan (TIDP)Each task team's list of what it will make, and whenEach team's page of the parts list
Master information delivery plan (MIDP)All the TIDPs joined up by the lead appointed partyThe whole parts list
