The scene

Goods In at the Site Office

Every parcel on the project passes through Goods In at the Site Office, the project's CDE. The sorters never open the boxes. They read the label and send each parcel down the right line. A parcel with no label can't go anywhere, so it ends up in the in-tray, where nobody will ever look again.

Why it matters
Without it, four files called FINAL all look equally right.

This is Metadata!

Metadata is information about information. It lets the town sort, find and trust a parcel without opening it.

ISO 19650 gives every information container a unique ID, built from an agreed naming convention, plus metadata for its status, revision and classification. In the UK, the naming fields come from the National Annex. That's the code on your label.

Sam's Heads Up Your labels may not look like this one

This example uses the seven fields many jobs start from: Project, Originator, Functional Breakdown, Spatial Breakdown, Form, Discipline and Number. That's the standard starting point, not a law of nature.

Your project sets its own naming convention, and it can add fields, rename them or change the codes. Some clients have ten-part names that read like a car registration crossed with a tax return. Always check your project's naming convention before you trust a label, or make one.

The codes on Drawing 042

Form D is a drawing (T would be text, L a list or schedule). Discipline C is civil engineering (Z means it covers several disciplines). XX means a field isn't subdivided, ZZ means it covers several. The Annex has the full lists of form and discipline codes, and your project's information standard says which ones your job uses.

Revision codes

CodeWhenWhat that means to you
P01.01, P01.02…Work in progressStill being worked on. The second number is the version within that revision, so the team can keep track of its working versions before the next shared revision. Nobody outside the team should be using it.
P01, P02…SharedA shared draft. The number goes up every time it's shared again, so check you've got the highest one.
C01, C02…PublishedThe published version, authorised and accepted. Higher is newer. Build from the latest C, with an A status.

An S code says what something may be used for. An A code only says it's been authorised and accepted, and the n is the work stage. The Annex ties n to the work stages in BS 8536-1 (buildings) and BS 8536-2 (infrastructure), since brought together in BS 8536:2022, unless your project's information standard sets out another approach. On this site, construction is stage 5 and handover is stage 6, so a construction issue is A5 and the as-built record is A6. Why each A code was issued isn't in the code at all: your project's information standard has to say.
