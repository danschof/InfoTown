Now there is: The hazard list (the health and safety file), built through the job.

What's going on here

The hazard list is information for the people who'll climb down into the station for the next fifty years. It has to be collected as the job changes, and checked against what was built, because the design that started the job isn't the station that finished it.

New at this stop

The hazard list
What the owner needs to know to run and maintain the station safely. (The health and safety file.)
In the town See it in the town: what does the person at the top of the ladder need to know?

The hazard list is information made for people who haven't arrived yet: whoever climbs down the wet well in five or fifty years. It should grow in the Site Office all year, picking up each change as it happens, like the wet well going two metres deeper and the cable nobody knew about. Before it leaves for Client Town it has to be checked against what was really built, just like the drawings are measured, not stamped. Built in the last week from year-old documents, it describes a station that no longer exists.

Fix it Safety file thin? Start filling it today.
  1. Name one person who owns the hazard list until handover.
  2. List every site change since design and check each is in it.
  3. Add hazard list updates to the weekly site meeting.

A note for the information manager: Put the hazard list on the parts list as a living deliverable with regular updates, not a single item due in the last week.
