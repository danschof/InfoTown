The back alley's bill so far: 72 weeks · See the bill

The information

Before anyone draws the new station, they need to know what's already there. I'm the survey. Use the new me, not the one in the archive.

The wrong way

Month one…
The new survey

Stevie's team finishes a fresh survey of the whole site: every level, every kerb, the new car park. He saves it to his laptop, in a folder called SURVEY_NEW.

The archive

Alex, the designer, asks for a survey. Someone finds one in the client's archive. It's five years old. Nobody checks the date.

Since then

Since that survey, the client has built a car park and raised the access road. The old survey has never heard of either.

The design

Alex designs the levels from it: the inlet, the access ramp, the drainage falls. Beautifully, and wrongly.

Month four…
The ramp

Rex: Is that meant to be a step?

The new ramp meets the road 400mm below it.

Redesign

Alex: We used the survey we had.

Stevie: Aye. That one's on me.

Levels, drainage and kerbs, all redesigned.

The right survey existed. It lived on Stevie's laptop, and the ramp met the road 400mm too low.

+4 weeks on the back alley's bill: A five-year-old survey: the ramp, levels and kerbs redesigned in month four.

The right way

Month one…
The new survey

Stevie's team finishes the fresh survey and puts it straight in the Site Office as the library copy everyone works from, with the date on it.

The old station

Stevie laser-scans the 1974 station the new one joins. The scan is 400GB, too big for the Site Office, so the Site Office keeps a card saying where it lives and how to get it. Not a drawer.

The design

Alex finds the survey in the Site Office, checks the date, and designs from it.

Month four…
The ramp

The ramp meets the road. Exactly. The van drives up it. Nobody notices, which is the point.

The survey on a laptopA five-year-old survey used instead, and a ramp 400mm short.
The library copyThe new survey in the Site Office, dated, and a ramp that meets the road.

Now there is: The library copy of the survey (reference information), and a card saying where the big scan lives (a record of where large data is kept).

What's going on here

Every design starts from what's already there. A survey on a laptop or a scan in a drawer is information nobody else can use. Put the newest survey where everyone finds it, with its date, and if something's too big to store there, record where it lives.

Try it on your job

Ask your designer which survey they're working from, and its date. Then ask where the newest one is.
