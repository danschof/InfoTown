The information

Week 8: the site opens. I need a home that works before anyone tries to live in it.

The wrong way

Day one…
Who turned up

Half the names in the bid are on the next bid. Nobody checks who's actually turned up.

The training

Training is an email with a link to a PDF. Nobody opens it.

Week two…
Testing on live drawings

The naming rules are tested on real drawings. Real drawings get the wrong names, and three go missing.

Everyone, to be safe

Access is set to "give everyone everything, to be safe". Rex can see the cost reports. So can a fencing firm that left in March.

Who gave them access?

Sam: Who gave the fencing firm the cost reports?

Everyone: IT.

The plan said train, test and control access. What happened was an email, live testing, and everyone, to be safe.

+3 weeks on the back alley's bill: A Site Office opened untested: three lost drawings, a fencing firm with the cost reports, and three weeks putting it right.

The right way

Week minus four…
Tested with dummy files

The Site Office is tested with dummy files, not real drawings. Things break where it doesn't matter.

Training, then logins

People get their logins after an hour's training, subcontractors included. Rex is shown the town: the buildings, the routes, where everything lives.

Goods In

Megan opens Goods In: the check at the gate that keeps the Site Office tidy. Anything named wrongly is sent back with a note.

Week two…
The right keys

Each firm sees what it needs. The fencing firm, when it leaves, stops seeing anything at all.

Ready before day oneDummy files, an hour's training, Goods In open and the right doors locked.

Now there is: Building it all before day one (mobilisation): a tested Site Office, trained people, the right access, and Goods In open.

What's going on here

The Site Office has to work before anyone relies on it. Testing it with dummy files, training people before they get access, giving each firm only what it needs, and opening Goods In before the first drawing arrives is what the plan for day one was for.
