The right way

Before handover…
Written with them

Wes, the commissioning engineer, writes the procedures with the pump team: what to do when, in the order you'd do it at 2am.

On the wall, practised

The procedures go on the plant room wall, where the operator can reach them, and in the client's own system. The night operators practise twice. Once with the lights off.

Now there is: Procedures where the operators need them, practised before handover.

What's going on here

Information is only useful where the person who needs it can reach it, at the moment they need it. For a pumping station that's the plant room wall at 2am. Writing procedures with the people who'll use them, and practising before handover, is information management for the night shift.

In the town See it in the town: 2.14am, and the information has to be in the room. Is it?

The builders have gone, and the station now belongs to Client Town. The information that matters most tonight is a short procedure, and it is only useful where the night operator can reach it: on the plant room wall, and in the client's own systems. A procedure buried in a portal nobody can log into is like a sign locked inside a cupboard. Written with the pump team and practised before handover, it is the data the owner runs on doing what it was for.

Fix it Can the night shift reach it? Check tonight.
  1. Ask an operator to find the failed-pump procedure without help.
  2. Put the urgent procedures on the plant room wall.
  3. Run a drill before handover, once with the lights off.

A note for the information manager: Test every handover item by asking who will use it, where and when, because a perfect file in the wrong place at 2am is no file at all.

Try it on your job

If something failed on your last finished job at 2am, where would the operator find what to do?
