End of the line

Six stages, a few questions each, picked at random from the stops on the line, so no two trips are the same. Tap a card, pick A or B, and find out straight away. Finish a stage to unlock the next.

  1. Win the work · Stop 1 · Should we bid?

    The go or no-go meeting covers price, programme and risk. What's missing?

    If nobody reads the information requirements before deciding to bid, nobody prices them either.

  2. Win the work · Stop 1 · Should we bid?

    The client wants its own system used, and we've never used it. At go or no-go, do you mention it?

    A system you have to learn is a cost. Better the board hears it now than the project finds it later.

  3. Win the work · Stop 2 · The client's wish list

    Nine hundred pages of tender. Where is what the client needs from information?

    Requirements hide in specifications, appendices and the contract. One list is how you find them all.

  4. Win the work · Stop 2 · The client's wish list

    Helen asks the board a simple question about her own division and gets three answers. What's wrong?

    An organisation needs its own information requirements, not just each project's.

  5. Win the work · Stop 3 · Whose system is it anyway?

    Which system will be the Site Office, the project's CDE?

    Your Records Office is your own organisation's CDE. The Site Office belongs to the project.

  6. Win the work · Stop 4 · Who's actually doing this?

    Three electrical firms quote, and each has priced different information. Why?

    Same enquiry pack, same information requirements, or you can't compare the quotes.

  7. Win the work · Stop 4 · Who's actually doing this?

    The bid says the information work will be done by "the team". Is that enough?

    When everyone is responsible, nobody is.

  8. Win the work · Stop 5 · The promise

    What is the pre-appointment BEP?

    The BEP is the promise. Copy the requirements back and you haven't promised anything.

  9. Win the work · Stop 6 · Can they actually do it?

    A firm in the team says it can manage information. What do you check?

    A claim is not evidence. Ask to see it.

  10. Win the work · Stop 7 · Pricing the information work

    Information management is priced as a percentage somebody used last time. Is that all right?

    The plug number was half what the job needed.

  11. Win the work · Stop 7 · Pricing the information work

    The price is too high, and the cost plan has an information line nobody can explain. Cut it?

    Cheap at tender, expensive on site.

  12. Win the work · Stop 8 · The plan for day one

    The plan for day one says "set up the CDE". Is that enough?

    A plan with no names and no dates is a wish.

  13. Win the work · Stop 9 · What could go wrong?

    Where should information risks go?

    Information risks are project risks.

  14. Win the work · Stop 10 · What we sent

    Submission morning: five versions called FINAL and three people editing. What should have happened?

    If you can't say what you sent, you can't say what you promised.

  15. Win the work · Stop 11 · Signed, sealed

    Helen signs the contract. Where are the information promises?

    If it's in what you signed, you're on the hook for it.

  16. Win the work · Stop 11 · Signed, sealed

    When are the BEP, the responsibility matrix and the delivery plans prepared?

    The plans form part of the appointment, so they come before the signature.

  1. Get ready · Stop 12 · Bid handover

    The bid wins and the bid team moves on. What should the delivery team get?

    Commitments nobody hands over are commitments nobody keeps.

  2. Get ready · Stop 13 · Which one's the real one?

    The client has a CDE, so do we, so does the designer. Which is the Site Office?

    Two places both claiming to be the real one is how you get two "latest" drawings.

  3. Get ready · Stop 13 · Which one's the real one?

    Rex arrives on site on day one and nothing is set up. Where should project information live?

    Information that starts life on a laptop tends to stay there.

  4. Get ready · Stop 14 · The plan grows up

    We've won. What happens to the plans in the bid?

    The plan that won the job is the plan you now have to deliver.

  5. Get ready · Stop 15 · Requirements to every firm

    When should a new firm's logins and training be ready?

    No login on Monday means everything comes by email, off the record.

  6. Get ready · Stop 17 · Who makes what, and when

    The delivery plan was last opened at tender. Does it matter?

    If it isn't on the plan, it's on nobody's list.

  7. Get ready · Stop 17 · Who makes what, and when

    A keen design team draws something nobody asked for. What should have stopped it?

    Demand first: what's needed, by whom, and why.

  8. Get ready · Stop 18 · Ready before day one

    Mobilisation: who needs training before they get a login?

    Untrained users find their own routes, usually the back alleys.

  9. Get ready · Stop 18 · Ready before day one

    Rex's first week: nobody shows him where anything lives. Whose job is that?

    If nobody shows you the town, you invent your own.

  10. Get ready · Stop 19 · One model of the whole job

    Whose coordinates should every model and survey use?

    Four sets of coordinates is how a pump room ends up in a field.

  1. Design it · Stop 20 · What's already in the ground

    What makes sure the designers use the new survey?

    The designers used a survey from five years ago because that's the one they could find.

  2. Design it · Stop 20 · What's already in the ground

    A 400GB laser scan is too big for the shared system. Where does it go?

    A file nobody knows about may as well not exist.

  3. Design it · Stop 21 · Made to the rules

    Four files called FINAL. What tells you which is which?

    Dates lie. Labels, used properly, don't.

  4. Design it · Stop 21 · Made to the rules

    Where do the project's naming and production rules come from?

    One rulebook, or four house styles.

  5. Design it · Stop 22 · Checked, actually

    Rex has finished his first drawing. Who checks it?

    Check, review and approve happen inside the team, before anything is shared.

  6. Design it · Stop 22 · Checked, actually

    Drawing 042 is finished at five to five on a Friday. It's wanted tonight. What's right?

    A tick is a claim. A check is evidence.

  7. Design it · Stop 22 · Checked, actually

    Alex puts 40 drawings in one PDF to save everyone some clicks. Problem?

    One PDF of 40 drawings is 40 drawings nobody can find.

  8. Design it · Stop 23 · The night before

    When do all the firms' models get looked at together?

    Problems found the night before become problems on site.

  9. Design it · Stop 24 · Questions to the designer

    Rex asks the designer a question in a phone chat and gets the right answer. What's missing?

    The right answer, kept in one phone, is the wrong answer for everybody else.

  10. Design it · Stop 25 · Authorised and accepted

    Who authorises a design, and who accepts it?

    Approve, authorise, accept: three checkpoints, three different owners.

  11. Design it · Stop 26 · Shop drawings

    A supplier's shop drawing comes in. Who is usually the accepting party?

    Who accepts depends on whose appointment the drawing belongs to.

  1. Build it · Stop 27 · Ordered off the current revision

    The specification changed a fortnight ago. What should the order be placed against?

    Ordered off the old spec means delivered to the old spec.

  2. Build it · Stop 28 · Briefing and permits

    The method statement is at C02. The copy on the cabin wall is C01. What do you do?

    The gang builds from what they're told, not from what's current.

  3. Build it · Stop 28 · Briefing and permits

    Rex's crane pad calculation has a tick. The sums are perfect, the inputs aren't. What should a check look at?

    "Show me your working."

  4. Build it · Stop 29 · Hold points

    The concrete is booked for Friday at 7am. The inspection isn't. What's missing?

    Once it's poured, nobody can inspect what's inside.

  5. Build it · Stop 30 · Found on site

    The licence for the protected newts is in an appendix. The digger is in the pond. What would have helped?

    Information nobody uses is information nobody has.

  6. Build it · Stop 30 · Found on site

    The ground is worse than the survey said. What does the contract need?

    Miss the notice and you can miss the money.

  7. Build it · Stop 31 · Site records

    Forty signed inspection sheets live in one red binder in the cabin, next to a river. What's better?

    One binder, one shelf, one flood.

  8. Build it · Stop 31 · Site records

    Site fixes a problem by lunchtime and tells nobody. What's lost?

    If it isn't written down, it didn't happen.

  9. Build it · Stop 31 · Site records

    Monitors spot movement on Tuesday. The people who could act find out eight days later. What's missing?

    Data nobody reads is just a number in a folder.

  10. Build it · Stop 32 · Changes and instructions

    The client asks for extra asset data by email, and the team just does it. Problem?

    Free work has a habit of becoming expected work.

  11. Build it · Stop 33 · Late, not extra

    A delay claim hangs on when a drawing reached site. The proof is in six inboxes. What should exist?

    The claim you can't prove is the claim you don't get.

  12. Build it · Stop 34 · Valuations

    Rex measured from C02. The wet well is now on C03. Why is Kofi's valuation wrong?

    Measure from what's current, or measure twice.

  13. Build it · Stop 34 · Valuations

    Payment is tied to accepted deliverables, and ours keep coming back rejected. What helps?

    No acceptance, no cash.

  14. Build it · Stop 35 · The monthly report

    The dashboard looks lovely. What should Helen ask?

    A dashboard is only as good as what's feeding it.

  15. Build it · Stop 36 · It got out

    Drawings of a pumping station that serves 40,000 homes leave the building. What should have stopped it?

    Some information needs a locked door, not just a route.

  16. Build it · Stop 37 · The drawing in the van

    042 has been reissued, and Dave's van copy is C01. What should happen when a new revision is published?

    The newest drawing is useless if the old one is still in the van.

  17. Build it · Stop 37 · The drawing in the van

    Site builds from an old drawing. Everyone says it isn't their fault. What's the fix?

    Whose job is it? Write it down before it goes wrong.

  18. Build it · Stop 38 · Audit rehearsal

    Priya books an audit rehearsal four months out, and the project keeps moving it. Why hold it?

    A rehearsal is cheaper than a major finding.

  19. Build it · Stop 39 · Audit day ★

    The assessor says "show me". What do they want to see?

    A process nobody follows isn't a process.

  20. Build it · Stop 39 · Audit day ★

    When does an assessor get most out of visiting a job?

    No work, no evidence.

    Drawn is not the same as built.

  2. Hand it over · Stop 42 · Manuals and asset data

    Ask for it when you order, or chase it for ever.

  3. Hand it over · Stop 43 · The safety file

    A year of safety information gets put together in the last week. What's better?

    Last-week files are made of whatever the inboxes cough up.

  4. Hand it over · Stop 44 · Signed out, signed for

    2,000 files is not a handover. It's a search problem.

  5. Hand it over · Stop 44 · Signed out, signed for

    What is the asset information the client keeps?

    The project model serves the job. The asset model serves the next fifty years.

  6. Hand it over · Stop 45 · It's all on the drive

    The job's handed over and everyone moves on. What's left to do?

    Left on the drive, it gets tidied away before the defect claim arrives.

  7. Hand it over · Stop 45 · It's all on the drive

    A supplier finishes and leaves, but can still log in. Problem?

    An open door is still an open door after the job.

  8. Hand it over · Stop 45 · It's all on the drive

    Bad information is costing the job a fortune, and nobody can say how much. Why?

    What nobody measures, nobody fixes.
