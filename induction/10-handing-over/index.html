The right way, explained

Shared isn't finished. The firm leading the work checks the whole delivery and authorises it. The client checks it's what they asked for and accepts it. Only what's on the parts list goes to the client. Everything else stays with you.

The scene

From the shared table to the published shelf

Parcels on the shared table carry S codes: shared, and fit for something. To become published, they make one more trip. The firm leading the work authorises the delivery, the client accepts it, and the S becomes an A. If it isn't right, it comes back with comments. And some parcels never make the trip at all. Watch out for

This is Authorisation & Acceptance!

Moving information from shared to published: authorised by the firm leading the work, then accepted by the client.

In ISO 19650 the lead appointed party reviews and authorises the information model (everything being handed over, taken together) for submission, then the appointing party reviews and accepts it, and it moves to the published state. In the UK, status codes show the journey: S4 when it's shared for the lead to authorise, S5 when it's shared for the client to accept, then an A code once it's authorised and accepted, numbered for the work stage. Only the information in the delivery plan needs to make the trip.

Sam's Heads Up S and A codes are the UK way, not the only way

The S0 to S5 and A codes here are widely used. Plenty of clients use them. Plenty don't, and some invent new ones halfway through the job.

Check your project's status codes before you share, authorise or build anything. "Approved" on one job can mean "please don't build this yet" on another.

Sam's Heads Up Every status is a promise

Share something "for coordination" and you're telling everyone they can build their work on yours, and you're standing behind it. Share it "for information" and you're only saying: here's where we've got to.

So pick the status for what it's really fit for, not for whatever gets it off your desk. And if it reaches you marked for information, don't build on it.

Fix it Handover coming up?

Handover
coming up?

  1. List what the parts list says the client gets. Only that.
  2. Check each parcel's S code, then get the delivery authorised.
  3. Agree with the client how they'll accept it, and write it down.

A note for the Coordinators (that's IM): plan every delivery milestone on the parts list on day one: what the client gets, when, who authorises it and how the client will accept it. Handover is the biggest milestone, not the only one. Handover is a journey, not a dump truck!

Revision codes

CodeWhenWhat that means to you
P01, P02…SharedA shared draft. The number goes up every time it's shared again, so check you've got the highest one.
C01, C02…PublishedThe published version, authorised and accepted. Higher is newer. Build from the latest C, with an A status.

An S code says what something may be used for. An A code only says it's been authorised and accepted, and the n is the work stage. The Annex ties n to the work stages in BS 8536-1 (buildings) and BS 8536-2 (infrastructure), since brought together in BS 8536:2022, unless your project's information standard sets out another approach. On this site, construction is stage 5 and handover is stage 6, so a construction issue is A5 and the as-built record is A6. Why each A code was issued isn't in the code at all: your project's information standard has to say.
