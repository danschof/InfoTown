Bev's view: the label says which revision is in the box. The order should say the same.

The scene

Goods In at the Site Office

Every parcel on the project passes through Goods In at the Site Office, the project's CDE. The sorters never open the boxes. They read the label and send each parcel down the right line. A parcel with no label can't go anywhere, so it ends up in the in-tray, where nobody will ever look again.

Labelled. Sorted and sent to the right place.
No label. Stuck in the in-tray.

Is it the latest?

P03 is the third preliminary revision. While it's being worked on it also carries a version, like P03.02. When it becomes part of the contract it starts again as C01.

An order is an Information Exchange too!

A purchase order carries information, the specification and drawings with their revisions, and it has to be current.

Sam's Heads Up Long-lead items move after you order them

The standard way: Orders are placed against the current revision.

On your job: On long-lead items the design can still change after the order. Agree with the designer which documents are frozen when you order, and who tells you if they move.

From the standard
ISO 19650 Revisions on everything

ISO 19650 expects information to carry its revision and status, and people to work from the current one. Using an order as the example is InfoTown's; how you place orders is your procurement process.

Based on the ISO 19650 series: the ideas come from the standard; the plain-English wording, examples and advice are InfoTown's. ISO 19650 is © ISO and BSI.
