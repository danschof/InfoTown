In the town See it in the town: three weeks late, and no proof. Where should the proof have been?

Kofi's view: the Site Office records everything in and out. That record is the evidence.

Now mobilise it: build and connect the site

In the middle sits the Site Office: the project's CDE, joined to your own Records Office by the home line. Lines run out to every town: the design line to the designers, the client line to the client, the home line back to your own Records Office, the feeder line to the site apps and footpaths to the cabins. The workflows running along all these lines are how one system passes work to another. Signposts tell everyone where each kind of work goes, and the gate decides who gets in.

The towns are there. The project site is an empty plot. Your job: mobilise it.

Feeder line. What's captured on site must reach the Site Office: by hand, on a schedule, through a link between systems, or because the app is part of the CDE.

Sam's Heads Up The CDE can't remember what it never saw

InfoTown good practice: Everything shared goes through the CDE, so it's on the record.

On your job: Some things will still arrive by email. Put them in the CDE the same day, with the date they arrived, or the record has a hole exactly where you'll need it.

For the audit What the auditor will ask
  • Show me when this drawing was received, and who was told.
  • How do you deal with information that arrives outside the CDE?
For the audit What good evidence looks like
  • A CDE record of each exchange, with dates and revisions.
  • A rule for logging anything that arrives another way, and proof it's followed.

From the standard
ISO 19650 The record of every exchange

ISO 19650 expects the CDE to keep a record of every exchange: what, which revision, who and when. Using that record in a claim is InfoTown's example; the claim itself is your contract's business.

Based on the ISO 19650 series: the ideas come from the standard; the plain-English wording, examples and advice are InfoTown's. ISO 19650 is © ISO and BSI.
