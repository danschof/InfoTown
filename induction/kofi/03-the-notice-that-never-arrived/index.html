Think like Kofi

A contract notice is information with a deadline and an address. Who has to be told, by when, how, and where's the proof they got it? If you're not sure it's needed, send it now and argue later.

The exchange map

Which line, which parcels, and when

Every project needs a map of its proper routes: who sends what, to whom, and when. Drawings come in on the design line, go out to site on the site line, and up to the client for acceptance. If it's on the map, everyone knows where to look. The back alley never is. Want to see a real job ride it? Follow the Process.

A contract notice needs a record too!

Something sent to a named person, through an agreed route, on time, with a record that it arrived.

Sam's Heads Up Your contract has its own words

InfoTown good practice: Send it through the agreed route to the named person, on time.

On your job: Your contract may call them early warnings, notices of delay or something else, each with its own deadline. Read yours; the deadline is the bit that bites.

For the audit What the auditor will ask
  • Show me the notices sent on this job, and proof each one arrived.
  • How does site know when something needs a notice?
For the audit What good evidence looks like
  • A notice log with dates, recipients and acknowledgements.
  • The contract's notice list, kept current with names.

From the standard
ISO 19650 Agreed routes

ISO 19650 expects information to be exchanged through agreed routes, with a record of what was shared, by whom and when. Notices and their deadlines are set by your contract; treating them as information exchanges is InfoTown's advice.

Based on the ISO 19650 series: the ideas come from the standard; the plain-English wording, examples and advice are InfoTown's. ISO 19650 is © ISO and BSI.
